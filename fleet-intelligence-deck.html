The problem

Fleet data arrives.
Insight doesn't.

  • Systems don't talk to each other.Telemetry, weather, traffic, and parcel data sit in separate systems with no shared, real-time view.
  • Maintenance is reactive.An engine issue is caught at breakdown, not at the moment it started trending wrong.
  • Safety review happens weekly.A harsh-braking pattern gets flagged in Friday's report — five days after it mattered.
  • Dispatch reacts by phone call.A route gets manually rerouted hours after a delay has already cascaded through the day.
By the time a report says "this vehicle needs attention," it needed attention yesterday.
Why real-time

Batch tells you what happened.
Streaming tells you what's happening.

Batch ETL
Nightly job, morning report
Data lands in a warehouse on a schedule. By the time anyone queries it, the moment it describes is already hours or days old.
VS
Event streaming
Every reading is an event
The instant a vehicle reports, that's a record on the stream — available to every consumer immediately, not on a schedule.

And because each business domain reads the same stream independently — safety, delivery, and maintenance don't wait on each other, or on a shared database, to react.

Why Confluent

One durable log.
Every team builds on it independently.

  • Managed KafkaThe durable event log every downstream system reads from — no producer coupled to any one consumer.
  • Schema RegistryEvery topic's shape is enforced and versioned, so producers and consumers evolve independently without breaking each other.
  • Flink SQLStream processing written as SQL — not a bespoke consumer application per team, per language.
  • Native AI Model callsOpenAI invoked directly from a Flink SQL statement — no separate inference service to build, deploy, or scale.
Confluent isn't just where the data lands — it's where the business logic and the AI inference actually run.
The solution

One raw stream. Three domains.
Real AI at the end of each.

Raw vehicle telemetry — plus weather, traffic, and parcel-volume context — feeds a continuous Flink SQL pipeline that derives three independent business events: safety, delivery, and maintenance. Each event is handed to an OpenAI model for a genuinely generated recommendation, published back to Kafka as its own topic — readable by a dashboard, a downstream system, or an MCP client (Claude Code/Desktop, VS Code Copilot), identically.
